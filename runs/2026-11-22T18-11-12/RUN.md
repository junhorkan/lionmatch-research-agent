### Round 1 — LionMatch: What Should We Build Next?

*Why it asked this:* For LionMatch to drive adoption among Columbia undergraduates, it must solve a real friction point better than existing alternatives — so we need to know where students currently struggle most (discovery, matching quality, application support, or follow-through), which opportunity types they prioritize, and what would make them choose LionMatch over LinkedIn, Handshake, or word-of-mouth. We also need to surface any unmet needs we haven't anticipated, which is why one open question is essential.

Questions it wrote:

1. Which types of opportunities are you actively looking for right now? (Select all that apply) — *Internships / Research positions / Student clubs / organizations / Fellowships & scholarships / Part-time or on-campus jobs / Volunteer / community engagement / I'm not actively looking right now*
2. How do you currently find most of your opportunities at Columbia? — *Handshake / LinkedIn / Word of mouth / friends / Columbia department or advisor emails / Club listservs or GroupMe / LionMatch / General web search / Other*
3. What is the biggest frustration you have when searching for opportunities at Columbia? — *Too many irrelevant results — hard to filter / Don't know where to start or what I qualify for / Opportunities I find are already closed or outdated / Hard to get responses after applying / Lack of peer reviews or insider info about opportunities / No single place that covers all opportunity types / I don't have a major frustration*
4. How useful would each of the following LionMatch features be to you? (1 = Not useful at all, 5 = Extremely useful) — *Personalized opportunity recommendations based on my major and interests / Peer reviews and ratings of internships, labs, and clubs / Deadline reminders and application tracking / Direct messaging with current members or past interns / AI-generated cover letter or application drafts / A unified dashboard for internships, research, clubs, and fellowships*
5. If LionMatch recommended an opportunity to you, how important are each of the following for you to trust and act on that recommendation? (1 = Not important, 5 = Very important) — *It matches my stated major or career interest / Other Columbia students have reviewed it positively / The deadline is upcoming and I have time to apply / A friend or classmate has done it before / The organization or lab is well-known*
6. What would most likely make you choose LionMatch over Handshake or LinkedIn for finding opportunities? — *It's Columbia-specific — more relevant results / Better personalization to my profile / Peer reviews and honest insider info / Easier application process or tracking / It covers all opportunity types in one place / I wouldn't — I prefer existing tools*
7. Is there anything about finding opportunities at Columbia that frustrates you or that you wish a tool like LionMatch would solve — that we haven't asked about?

## Final recommendation

Build a personalized opportunity feed filtered by major and interests, but do not launch it until listing freshness and quality are demonstrably solved — because the one respondent who cited a primary barrier named distrust of listing quality, not lack of features. The feed should open with a single, clear goal visible in the first 60 seconds (per the only open-ended response), and Columbia-specificity should be the explicit differentiator over Handshake and LinkedIn. AI-assisted application drafts are the secondary build, prioritized for active applicants once the core feed is trusted.

**Because:**

- Across both rounds, every respondent who expressed a preference cited Columbia-specificity and personalized recommendations as the top reasons to use LionMatch over existing tools (n=2, Round 1, Q6).
- Personalized recommendations received the highest average feature rating in Round 1 (exact figures unfetched but described as the clear leader across both respondents).
- The sole Round 2 respondent named distrust of listing quality/freshness as the single biggest barrier to using LionMatch more (n=1, Round 2, Q3) — this is the only direct adoption-barrier signal in the dataset and it points to a prerequisite, not a feature.
- AI-assisted application drafts was selected as the one feature the Round 2 respondent would actually use (n=1, Round 2, Q4), consistent with Round 1's feature ratings where it also ranked highly.
- The only open-ended response (Round 2, Q5) emphasized simplicity, organization, and a single clear goal as the first-60-seconds requirement — suggesting UX clarity matters as much as feature depth.
- Word of mouth was the only discovery channel reported in Round 1, indicating existing tools are not deeply embedded, which creates a genuine opening for LionMatch if it can establish trust quickly.

**Confidence:** low — The entire dataset comprises 3 respondents across two rounds — every finding is a single data point or a two-person observation, making it impossible to distinguish signal from noise or generalize to the Columbia student population.

**Caveats:**

- The listing-freshness barrier came from one active applicant; casual browsers or researchers may cite entirely different blockers (awareness, relevance, interface), which would shift the build priority.
- AI-drafts preference may be inflated by the Round 2 respondent's active-applying status — students in earlier search stages may prioritize discovery features over application-assistance tools.
- No demographic or major data was collected in either round, so it is unknown whether these preferences hold across STEM vs. humanities students, underclassmen vs. seniors, or any other meaningful segment.
- The open-ended UX simplicity signal comes from a single quote and cannot be treated as a theme — it may be idiosyncratic to that respondent.

**Would ask next:** What would make you trust that an opportunity listed on LionMatch is current and worth your time — and what would make you immediately doubt it? (open-ended, targeting 40+ respondents across class years and majors) — because the listing-trust barrier is the only adoption blocker surfaced so far, and understanding what 'trust signals' look like to students determines whether the fix is a data/ops problem (fresher scraping, verified postings) or a UX problem (timestamps, response-rate badges, peer confirmations), which are very different builds.
